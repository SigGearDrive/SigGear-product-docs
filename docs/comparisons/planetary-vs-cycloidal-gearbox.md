# Planetary vs Cycloidal Gearbox

Planetary and cycloidal gearboxes are both widely used in compact motion systems. The correct choice depends on torque, speed, backlash, impact conditions, size, weight, efficiency, control requirements and production goals.

There is no single transmission type that is best for every robot application.

## Quick Comparison

| Factor | Planetary Gearbox | Cycloidal Gearbox |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Main advantage | Compact, high speed, flexible motor integration | High torque density and strong shock-load capability |
| Typical use | Robot arms, compact actuators, automation axes | High-load robot joints and demanding motion systems |
| Output speed | Usually higher | Usually lower for equivalent torque |
| Reduction ratio | Wide range | Commonly high reduction ratios |
| Backlash | Depends on design and precision level | Depends on design and preload configuration |
| Shock resistance | Good when properly selected | Often suitable for high impact environments |
| Weight | Often compact for lower torque ranges | Can be heavier for similar packaging |

## Planetary Gearbox Characteristics

Planetary gearboxes use sun gears, planet gears and a ring gear to transmit motion.

Advantages:

- Compact coaxial structure
- High input speed capability
- Flexible ratio selection
- Easy motor matching
- Suitable for servo motor integration

Common applications:

- Robotic arms
- Collaborative robots
- Compact rotary axes
- AGV mechanisms
- Automation equipment

## Cycloidal Gearbox Characteristics

Cycloidal reducers use a cycloidal disc and pin mechanism to transmit torque.

Advantages:

- High torque capability
- Strong resistance to shock loads
- High reduction ratios
- Large contact area between components

Common applications:

- Humanoid robot joints
- Quadruped robot joints
- Heavy robot axes
- High-load automation

## Selection Questions

Choose based on:

- Required continuous torque
- Peak torque and duration
- Output speed
- Reduction ratio
- Backlash requirement
- External radial and axial loads
- Weight limit
- Duty cycle
- Motor and controller architecture
