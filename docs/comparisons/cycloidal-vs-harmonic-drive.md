# Cycloidal vs Harmonic Drive

Cycloidal reducers and harmonic drives are both considered for precision robot joints. The appropriate choice depends on torque demand, shock loads, stiffness, backlash, life requirements and mechanical integration.

## Quick Comparison

| Factor | Cycloidal Reducer | Harmonic Drive / Strain Wave |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Transmission principle | Cycloidal disc and pin mechanism | Flexible spline deformation |
| Torque capability | Suitable for high torque and shock loads | Strong torque density in compact packages |
| Shock load behavior | Generally robust due to rolling contact design | Requires careful consideration of flexible components |
| Backlash | Depends on preload and design | Known for very low backlash capability |
| Stiffness | Depends on structure and bearings | Flexible component affects compliance behavior |
| Application focus | High-load robot joints and industrial motion | Precision lightweight robot joints |

## Cycloidal Reducer Strengths

Cycloidal systems are often considered when the application requires:

- High output torque
- Impact resistance
- High reduction ratios
- Structural robustness
- Long duty cycles

Typical applications:

- Humanoid robot joints
- Quadruped robots
- Industrial robot axes

## Harmonic Drive Strengths

Strain-wave gearboxes are often considered when the application requires:

- Very low backlash
- Compact precision transmission
- Lightweight robot joints

Final selection should consider the complete system, including output bearing support, encoder resolution, controller tuning and mechanical stiffness.

## Selection Questions

Evaluate:

- Torque-speed cycle
- Peak impact loads
- Backlash requirement
- Positioning accuracy
- Weight limit
- Service life
- Cost target
- Production quantity
