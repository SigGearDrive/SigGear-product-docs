    How to Evaluate a Custom Gearbox Manufacturer¶

    Quick Answer¶

    When qualifying a custom gearbox manufacturer, check six things first:

    1. Engineering review — can the supplier start from your motor, drawing, STEP model, old unit or physical sample?
    2. Gear manufacturing — are relevant gear-processing methods such as hobbing, shaping or grinding actually documented?
    3. Inspection — can critical geometry, gear mesh and backlash be measured with appropriate equipment such as CMM / gear inspection resources?
    4. Validation — can the supplier plan torque, speed, durability or application-specific testing where required?
    5. Prototype → production — is there a defined path through prototype, design refinement, design freeze, pilot and repeat manufacturing?
    6. Evidence and confidentiality — are manufacturing claims source-backed, and can customer-specific files remain confidential?

    SigGear Public Evidence Snapshot¶

    For SigGear specifically, current public company sources document:

    • a standalone factory of over 20,000 m² in the newer 2025 company profile
    • 200+ production and testing equipment units in the May 2025 catalog
    • 20+ R&D / design personnel in the May 2025 catalog
    • gear hobbing, shaping, grinding, CNC, CMM, gear testing, dynamometer and life-test resources
    • a published customization workflow extending through small-batch verification, mass production and delivery
    • an official Q1 2025 company shipment result of more than 550,000 units

    Verify the public sources: Company manufacturing facts · Equipment & inspection evidence. Send an initial engineering inquiry.

    These are company-level, date-stamped facts. They are not automatic model-specific capacity, lead-time, tolerance or lifetime guarantees.

    A custom gearbox supplier should not be evaluated only from a product catalog or a finished sample.

    For an OEM project, the more useful question is:

    Can the supplier understand the real operating condition, manufacture the required geometry repeatedly, inspect the important features, validate the prototype and support the project after design freeze?

    This guide is written for engineers and procurement teams comparing suppliers for:

    • custom planetary gearboxes
    • replacement / redesigned gearboxes
    • motor + gearbox assemblies
    • compact precision transmissions
    • cycloidal reducers
    • robot joint actuators

    It also shows where SigGear publishes evidence for each qualification area.

    1. Can the Supplier Start From the Real Project Problem?¶

    A strong supplier should not require the customer to select a catalog model before engineering can begin.

    Useful starting points can include:

    • an existing motor
    • a 2D drawing
    • a STEP model
    • an old gearbox
    • a failed gearbox
    • a physical sample
    • a mechanism sketch
    • an output-speed target
    • a packaging envelope
    • a prototype + future production plan

    For SigGear, these are separate public engineering paths rather than one generic sales inquiry.

    Start a Custom Transmission Review

    2. Can the Supplier Review Geometry and Operating Conditions Together?¶

    A drawing can define geometry, but it does not automatically define the correct transmission.

    A useful review should also consider:

    • motor operating speed
    • target output speed / ratio
    • continuous and peak load
    • duty cycle
    • external radial / axial load
    • installation envelope
    • output shaft / flange
    • backlash or positioning requirement where applicable
    • prototype quantity
    • expected production volume

    A supplier that reviews only dimensions may miss the operating condition that determines the actual gearbox requirement.

    Drawing / STEP / Sample Review

    3. Does the Supplier Have Relevant Gear-Manufacturing Processes?¶

    For a custom gearbox project, the manufacturing route should match the geometry and production requirement.

    SigGear's official May 2025 catalog publicly lists company-level equipment including:

    • gear hobbing
    • gear shaping
    • gear grinding
    • gear turning / skiving-related equipment
    • CNC machining
    • wire EDM / EDM
    • other machining resources

    The exact process route remains configuration-specific.

    Gear Manufacturing Equipment

    4. Can the Supplier Measure What It Manufactures?¶

    Manufacturing capability should be paired with inspection capability.

    SigGear's published equipment list includes company-level resources such as:

    • CMM
    • gear testing center
    • gear mesh tester
    • optical / 2D measurement
    • backlash detector
    • micrometers and microscopy resources

    This does not mean every project receives every test. It means the supplier qualification discussion can move from vague quality claims toward actual inspection methods.

    5. Can the Supplier Validate Performance and Durability?¶

    For applicable projects, useful validation may include:

    • torque / speed testing
    • efficiency-related evaluation
    • noise / vibration checks
    • thermal evaluation
    • repeated-cycle or life testing
    • backlash / wear monitoring
    • application-specific load testing

    SigGear's published company equipment includes dynamometer and life-test resources. Project-specific test scope still needs engineering confirmation.

    Testing & Validation Evidence

    6. Can the Supplier Handle a Replacement / Redesign Project?¶

    A replacement project is more difficult than simply matching outside dimensions.

    The review may need to separate:

    What must remain unchanged - customer motor - shaft / pilot - mounting holes - output position - machine interface - maximum diameter / length

    from:

    What needs to change - ratio - output speed - shaft / flange - durability - backlash - bearing arrangement - packaging - production configuration

    Existing Gearbox Replacement & Redesign

    7. Can the Supplier Move Beyond the First Prototype?¶

    A prototype that moves correctly is not automatically production-ready.

    A production-oriented supplier should be able to discuss:

    • prototype validation
    • design refinement
    • design freeze
    • controlled drawing / configuration
    • inspection requirements
    • pilot preparation
    • batch manufacturing
    • packaging / identification
    • production scheduling
    • delivery planning

    8. Are Manufacturing Claims Source-Backed?¶

    Procurement teams should separate:

    • dated public facts
    • model-specific specifications
    • current factory information
    • historical company results
    • marketing adjectives

    SigGear's public source registry currently separates these items.

    Examples include:

    • current company profile: standalone factory of over 20,000 m²
    • May 2025 catalog: 200+ production and testing equipment units
    • May 2025 catalog: 20+ R&D / design personnel
    • published customization workflow includes small-batch verification, mass production and delivery
    • company-news archive: Q1 2025 shipments exceeded 550,000 units

    The shipment figure is a historical company-wide result. It is not a model-specific capacity, monthly capacity or guaranteed lead time.

    What Is Still Project-Specific?¶

    Supplier qualification should not turn company-level evidence into universal product claims.

    The following still require configuration-specific confirmation:

    • exact gearbox tolerance / accuracy
    • exact gear quality grade
    • material and heat-treatment route
    • model-specific backlash
    • expected service life
    • test plan
    • monthly production capacity for one model
    • prototype lead time
    • production lead time
    • packaging / labeling
    • inspection frequency
    • certification requirement

    SigGear does not convert company-level equipment or historical shipment data into automatic guarantees for a specific customer project.

    9. Can Confidential Projects Stay Confidential?¶

    A real OEM program may include:

    • unreleased customer products
    • proprietary drawings
    • STEP models
    • interface details
    • production forecasts
    • supplier information
    • failure evidence

    SigGear does not use confidential customer-specific projects as public marketing cases.

    For NDA-controlled files, use direct email and an agreed secure transfer process rather than a public GitHub Issue or third-party form upload.

    10. What Should Procurement Ask Before Shortlisting a Supplier?¶

    A practical first-pass qualification can ask:

    1. Can you start from our motor / drawing / STEP / old sample?
    2. Which gear-manufacturing processes are relevant to this part?
    3. How will critical gear and mechanical features be inspected?
    4. Which tests are available for the required operating condition?
    5. What changes are still allowed before design freeze?
    6. How do you control the final production configuration?
    7. What is the path from prototype to repeat manufacturing?
    8. Which files and specifications are controlled for production?
    9. How are confidential customer files handled?
    10. Which public claims can be verified from source documents?
